# v1.6.58 Piano Transient Integrity Audit

## User symptom

A mastered piano melody can sound like a thin bell, torn foil, glass, or a distorted metallic edge even when the source itself sounds natural.

No original/mastered audio pair was included with this code review, so this patch does not claim a file-specific diagnosis. It addresses the engine paths that can independently create that symptom and adds telemetry to make a later file-level diagnosis decisive.

## Root-cause audit

### 1. Serial loudness drive

The offline render chain applied a loudness lift of up to +8.5 dB before the worker finalizer measured LUFS and applied another target gain. The first lift had no lasting loudness benefit because the second stage normalized the result again, but it could drive harmonic attacks into compression.

**Resolution:** pre-finalizer gain is now bounded staging only. Normal LUFS ownership belongs to the worker finalizer.

### 2. Serial limiting

The offline Web Audio chain used a 1 ms `DynamicsCompressorNode` as a limiter, then the worker applied its own lookahead True-Peak limiter. Piano attacks could therefore be reshaped twice.

**Resolution:** normal mastering bypasses the pre-finalizer limiter. It remains only as an emergency guard for custom intensity 180 or above. The worker is the authoritative limiter.

### 3. Near-ceiling waveshaping

After lookahead limiting, both worker and fallback code passed samples above 98.5% of the ceiling through a `tanh` soft ceiling. Repeated piano hammer peaks near the ceiling could receive non-linear harmonic generation even when a transparent global safety gain would have been sufficient.

**Resolution:** the per-sample soft ceiling stage was removed. Residual FIR True-Peak overshoot is corrected once by global gain.

### 4. High-frequency exciter interaction

The exciter high-passed around 5-7.2 kHz, applied a 4x oversampled WaveShaper, then added an air shelf. This is useful on dull sources but can turn a piano's naturally dense upper partials and hammer noise into a glassy or tearing edge.

**Resolution:** a melodic-transient glass-risk estimate now scales the drive and wet mix and bypasses the exciter when risk exceeds 46%.

### 5. Narrow static resonance cuts

Metallic removal used several high-Q peaking filters. Deep narrow cuts can reduce a stationary resonance but may produce phase rotation and ringing around short harmonic attacks.

**Resolution:** piano/glass risk reduces notch Q, depth, and aggregate metallic-removal aggression. The existing vocal risk guard remains active.

### 6. Tone and preview mismatch

Presence and high-shelf boosts could remain active while the offline exciter was being risk-scaled. The real-time preview also retained a stronger limiter profile.

**Resolution:** the same risk estimate now reduces preview presence, high shelf, notch Q, output gain, and limiter aggressiveness.

### 7. Program-independent limiter release

A fixed release can chatter on repeated upper-register notes or remain clamped into the sustain depending on tempo and note spacing.

**Resolution:** high melodic-transient risk adds up to 28 ms to limiter release in the worker and fallback, reducing rapid gain movement.

### 8. Missing positive-glare regression

The previous quality audit detected high-frequency loss but not excessive high-frequency creation.

**Resolution:** `HIGH_GLARE` is emitted when high-frequency proxy energy rises materially while crest factor collapses or envelope motion rises. The quality gate labels it as `고역 찢어짐/유리질`.

## Engine changes

- Added `estimateMelodicTransientGlassRisk()`.
- Reduced clarity, punch, analog groove, stereo groove, and extreme intensity when risk is high.
- Risk-scaled the high-frequency exciter and bypassed it at high risk.
- Broadened and reduced metallic-removal notches for transient-rich melodic material.
- Reduced high-frequency tone-chain aggression.
- Replaced strong pre-finalizer loudness lift with bounded staging.
- Made the worker finalizer the single normal limiting owner.
- Removed near-ceiling per-sample waveshaping.
- Added risk-adaptive limiter release.
- Added risk telemetry to shared DSP summary, worker output, master report, and quality gate.
- Added `HIGH_GLARE` detection.

## Repeated challenge questions and answers

1. **Could the source already contain the artifact?** Yes. Only an original/mastered pair can separate source resonance from engine-created distortion.
2. **Could MP3 encoding create it?** Yes, especially at lower bitrates. WAV24 must be checked first.
3. **Could Bluetooth or a phone speaker create it?** Yes. Wired headphones and a second playback device are required controls.
4. **Could clipping occur before the finalizer?** Previously yes; serial drive and limiting made that plausible. The patch reduces that path.
5. **Could True-Peak limiting itself be the cause?** It can be if reduction is excessive. Telemetry and gate thresholds remain active.
6. **Could the soft ceiling add harmonics?** Yes. It was non-linear and has been removed from the normal path.
7. **Could the exciter produce a bell-like edge?** Yes. Its WaveShaper creates harmonics and is now bypassed for high-risk piano-like material.
8. **Could metallic removal paradoxically sound metallic?** A narrow high-Q cut can ring or sound phasey on transients. Q and depth are now risk-scaled.
9. **Could stereo widening cause tearing?** It can create comb-like coloration after mono fold-down. Existing phase-safe limits remain and glass risk also reduces stereo groove.
10. **Could the analysis mistake piano harmonics for vocal sibilance?** Yes. Vocal-only heuristics were insufficient; a separate melodic-transient risk is now used.
11. **Could the analysis mistake piano for electronic content?** Yes. The patch does not rely solely on genre; it uses transient and spectral measurements.
12. **Could strong clarity settings still override protection?** Protection reduces the effective setting after goal/style/strength adjustments.
13. **Could custom mode bypass protection?** Smart Guard still applies, with the existing reduced custom guard behavior plus the new risk caps.
14. **Could intensity 180-200 still distort?** It remains an extreme mode. An emergency pre-finalizer guard is retained and quality-gate warnings remain possible.
15. **Could the finalizer pump the piano sustain?** Excessive reduction can. Risk-adaptive release reduces chatter, and active percentage/reduction are reported.
16. **Could removing the soft ceiling exceed the ceiling?** The final FIR scan applies global safety gain if the measured True Peak exceeds the target.
17. **Could global safety gain change tone?** It is linear and therefore does not create new harmonics.
18. **Could lower notch Q leave resonance behind?** Yes, but that trade-off is preferable to ringing. Dynamic de-essing and the final quality gate remain available.
19. **Could the patch make dull masters?** The guard activates progressively; low-risk material retains the existing exciter and tone behavior.
20. **Could the preview and final render still differ?** They use different APIs, but both now share the same effective settings and glass-risk direction.
21. **Could sample-rate conversion be responsible?** The main mastering render retains the decoded sample rate. Pitch/speed transformation remains a separate suspect when enabled.
22. **Could pitch or tempo changes cause metallic artifacts?** Yes. WSOLA/pitch processing must be tested with transforms disabled and enabled separately.
23. **Could the instrument layer be responsible?** It is only relevant when explicitly enabled; a clean test must disable added layers.
24. **Could browser implementations differ?** Yes. `DynamicsCompressorNode` and decoding can differ, which is another reason normal limiting now resides in deterministic worker DSP.
25. **Could the issue be only in playback, not the exported file?** Yes. The downloaded WAV should be inspected in an external player/DAW.
26. **Could stale service-worker assets mix engine versions?** Version synchronization and a new cache generation are required for deployment.
27. **Could the guard overreact to cymbals?** The risk is not an instrument classifier. It is bounded and primarily suppresses non-linear drive; electronic high-risk sources may also benefit.
28. **Could the guard miss a sparse solo piano with low average transient density?** Yes. File-level A/B and a future local transient-window detector would improve precision.
29. **Could a single resonant note escape whole-track analysis?** Yes. The current analysis is aggregate. A future patch can add time-local harshness maps.
30. **Is the issue conclusively fixed without the user's audio?** No. The highest-risk engine mechanisms are fixed and regression-tested, but source-specific confirmation still requires the exact pair.
